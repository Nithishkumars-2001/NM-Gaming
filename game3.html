How to Play Tic Tac Toe

Tic Tac Toe is a two-player game played on one screen. Players take turns placing X and O on a 3 × 3 grid. This guide covers the rules, the scoreboard and how to win.

1. The goal

Be the first player to get three of your marks in a straight line. If nobody manages it and the grid fills up, the round is a draw.

2. Getting started

  1. Two players sit at the same screen or phone. There is no computer opponent.
  2. X always goes first. The Turn chip at the top shows whose turn it is.
  3. Tap an empty square to place your mark.
  4. The turn passes automatically to the other player.
  5. Keep going until someone wins or the grid is full.

3. Controls

Mouse or touch
Click or tap an empty square to place your mark. Squares that are already taken ignore taps.
Turn chip
Shows X or O while the round is on, so you always know who plays next.
New Round
Clears the grid and starts a fresh round. Win counts are kept.

4. How a round ends

  • A win. Three matching marks in a row, a column or a diagonal. There are eight possible lines in total.
  • A draw. All nine squares are filled and nobody has a line. The Turn chip shows Draw.
  • After the round. The grid locks. Press New Round to play again.

When X or O wins, the Turn chip shows who won, for example X Wins!.

5. The scoreboard

The game keeps count of wins for each player while the page stays open.

Result Turn chip shows Scoreboard
X gets three in a row X Wins! X Wins goes up by 1
O gets three in a row O Wins! O Wins goes up by 1
Grid full, no line Draw No change

The scoreboard is not saved. If you refresh or close the page, both counts go back to 0.

6. Playing like a pro

  • Take the centre. The middle square is part of four different lines.
  • Corners are next best. They give you more ways to make a line than edge squares.
  • Always block. If the other player has two in a row, fill the third square right away.
  • Make a fork. Create two threats at once. The other player can only block one.
  • Watch the diagonals. They are easy to miss when you are focused on rows.
  • Expect draws. When both players play well, almost every round ends in a draw.

7. Common questions

Who goes first in the next round?X always starts, even if O won the last round.
Can I play against the computer?No. This version is for two players sharing one screen.
Are the win counts saved?No. They only last until you refresh or leave the page.
Can I undo a move?No. Once a mark is placed it stays until the round ends.
Why can't I tap a square?Either the square is already taken or the round has ended. Press New Round to start again.

🃏 Memory Match

A 4 × 4 board hides 8 pairs of symbols. Flip two cards at a time and find every pair.

  1. Tap a card to flip it and see the symbol.
  2. Tap a second card. If the two match, they stay face up.
  3. If they don't match, they flip back after a moment, so remember where they were.
  4. Clear all 8 pairs in as few moves as you can. Every two flips count as one move.

Pro tip: Scan the board in a fixed order so you can recall positions by row and column.

✊ Rock Paper Scissors

Pick your hand against the computer and see who wins the round. The score keeps counting as long as you play.

  1. Choose Rock, Paper or Scissors.
  2. Rock beats Scissors, Scissors beats Paper, Paper beats Rock.
  3. If both pick the same, the round is a draw and nobody scores.
  4. Scores and the round counter keep running. Refresh the page to reset them.

Pro tip: The computer picks at random, so no pattern can beat it. Set a target, like first to 5, to make it a mini match.

🔨 Whack-a-Mole

A 3 × 3 grid of holes and 30 seconds on the clock. Tap the mole each time it pops up.

  1. Press Start. A mole appears in one hole at a time.
  2. Tap the hole with the mole to score 10 points. Tapping an empty hole costs nothing.
  3. The mole moves faster as your score climbs.
  4. When the 30 seconds are up, your score is compared with your Best.

Pro tip: Watch the whole grid, not one hole, so you can jump to the next mole as soon as it appears.

🎯 Guess the Number

A secret number from 1 to 100 is hidden. Use the Higher or Lower hints to find it in the fewest guesses.

  1. Type a number from 1 to 100 and press Guess, or press Enter.
  2. You get a hint: Higher or Lower.
  3. Use the hint to narrow down the range and guess again.
  4. Find the number to win. Fewer attempts means a better Best score.

Pro tip: Always guess the middle of what is left. Starting at 50 finds any number in 7 guesses or fewer.

🔵 Simon Says

Four gold pads flash in a growing pattern. Watch, then repeat it from memory.

  1. Press Start and watch the pads flash. The Status chip says Watch.
  2. When it says Your Turn, tap the pads in the same order.
  3. Every correct round adds one more step to the pattern.
  4. One wrong tap ends the game. Best keeps the highest round you reached.

Pro tip: All four pads look the same, so remember positions (top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right) instead of colours.

Play Simon Says →

🔢 2048

Slide numbered tiles around a 4 × 4 board. Matching tiles merge into one bigger tile.

  1. Use the Arrow Keys, or swipe on a phone, to slide every tile in one direction.
  2. Two tiles with the same number merge into one, and your score goes up by the new number.
  3. A new 2 or 4 appears after every move that changes the board.
  4. Keep merging to build the 2048 tile.

Pro tip: Keep your biggest tile in one corner and avoid pressing the opposite direction.

Play 2048 →

🏓 Pong

The classic paddle duel against the CPU. Keep the ball in play and make the CPU miss.

  1. Press Start. The ball launches in a random direction.
  2. Move your mouse or finger up and down over the board to control your paddle on the left.
  3. If the ball gets past the CPU paddle, you score. If it gets past yours, the CPU scores.
  4. The ball gets 5% faster horizontally every time it hits a paddle.

Pro tip: The CPU paddle never speeds up but the ball does, so long rallies work in your favour.
